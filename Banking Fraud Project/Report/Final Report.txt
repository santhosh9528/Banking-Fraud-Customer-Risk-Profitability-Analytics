1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This project presents an end-to-end analytical study of a retail banking environment 
to identify suspicious transactions, evaluate customer and loan risk, analyze customer profitability,
and provide management with actionable business insights.

The analysis was performed using multiple banking datasets covering customers, accounts, transactions,
loans, loan payments, credit scores, branches, complaints, and login/device activity.

Python was used for data cleaning, fraud investigation, loan risk analysis, customer profitability analysis, 
and statistical testing. SQL was used for advanced banking analysis including transaction trends,
customer ranking, branch performance, loan defaults, repayment behavior, running totals, and month-over-month growth. 
Power BI was used to develop interactive Executive, Fraud, and Loan Risk dashboards.

After data preparation, the final analysis contained 999 valid customers and 26,168 valid transactions.

Fraud analysis identified 280 High/Critical suspicious transactions. The dataset contained 140 confirmed 
fraud transactions, and all 140 confirmed fraud records were captured by the High/Critical fraud-risk criteria. 
An additional 140 transactions were identified as suspicious but were not labeled as confirmed fraud.

Loan analysis identified 646 valid loans with an overall observed default rate of 21.67%. Statistical
 analysis found that defaulted customers had an average credit score of 677.80 compared with 699.52 for
 non-defaulted customers. The hypothesis test produced a p-value of 0.001083, indicating a statistically
 significant relationship between credit score and loan default.

Customer profitability analysis estimated approximately ₹29.40 million in modeled customer revenue, ₹2.96 million in operational costs, and ₹26.45 million in modeled customer profit.

Overall, the project demonstrates how Python, SQL, statistics, and Power BI can be integrated to support fraud detection, credit-risk management, customer profitability analysis, and management decision-making.

34. HYPOTHESIS TESTING

Null Hypothesis (H0):

There is no statistically significant difference in credit scores between defaulted and non-defaulted customers.

Alternative Hypothesis (H1):

There is a statistically significant difference in credit scores between defaulted and non-defaulted customers.

Welch Independent T-Test Results:

T-Statistic = -3.3091
P-Value = 0.001083
Significance Level = 0.05

Since:

P-Value < 0.05

Decision:

REJECT H0

Conclusion:

Credit score has a statistically significant relationship with loan default in the analyzed dataset.


35. POINT-BISERIAL CORRELATION

• Credit Score

• Interest Rate
• Days Past Due
• Repayment Rate

Regression R-Squared = 0.8205

Days past due showed the strongest positive relationship with default in the model.

Repayment rate showed a negative relationship with default.

The multivariable credit-score coefficient should be interpreted cautiously because credit score and interest rate showed strong correlation of approximately -0.908, 
indicating potential multicollinearity.

Because loan default is a binary outcome, logistic regression would generally be more appropriate for a production predictive model.

43. LIMITATIONS

• The datasets used in this project are synthetic and were created for analytical assessment purposes.

• Fraud Risk Scores and Loan Risk Scores are rule-based analytical scores rather than production machine-learning models.

• Confirmed fraud labels were used to validate the fraud framework.

• Known default status contributed to the Loan Risk Score, so the risk score should not be treated as an independently validated predictive model.

• Customer profitability includes estimated annual loan interest revenue and annualized service costs.

• Profitability results therefore represent modeled customer economics rather than audited realized profit.

• The regression used a linear probability model with a binary default outcome. Logistic regression would generally be more suitable for production default prediction.

• Strong correlation between some predictor variables can influence multivariable regression coefficients.
